# Economics and finance/crypto edges against external references, 2026-10-04

Research and paper only. Public data (Kalshi public API incl. historical tier; Cleveland Fed nowcast JSON; Atlanta Fed GDPNow workbook; Yahoo Finance ZQ fed-funds futures, ^GSPC/^NDX/^VIX/^VXN; NY Fed EFFR; Freddie Mac PMMS; Coinbase 5-min candles; Deribit public DVOL and live option chain). No orders, no account reads.
Layout: `scripts/` (re-runnable, `scripts/analyze.sh` gives the order), `results/` (tables, small), `data/` (raw, rows, candles; git-ignored by `data/.gitignore`, about 150 MB, regenerable).

Method, common to every test. Snapshot = last quoted bid/ask before a fixed time (evening before an economic release, or N minutes before a crypto/index close). Reference probability from the external source, built walk-forward (every spread, bias or vol scale uses only earlier data). Scores: Brier and log loss of Kalshi mid vs reference vs a logit blend; differences carry event-clustered bootstrap 95% CIs (clusters are releases, or days for crypto/index). Trading EV = taker fill at the displayed ask (YES) or at 1 - bid (NO), settled P&L minus Kalshi's taker fee `ceil(0.07*n*p*(1-p))` at n=10 lots, per contract, train/test split by date, parameters chosen on train only. Limits to read before any number: displayed depth is not in the candles, so taker fills at 10 lots are assumed, not verified; maker/queue-aware replay was done for one cell only (BTC, below); sample sizes are releases, and clustering makes them small.

## 1. Economics

### Fed decisions (KXFEDDECISION) vs fed-funds futures
Free data limit: Yahoo keeps only unexpired ZQ contracts (ZQU26 onward) plus the continuous front month ZQ=F (rolls at month start). For meetings with at least 8 days of the month left after the decision, the front-month price the day before gives the month-average rate, so the post-meeting rate follows from `((100-P)*N - EFFR*d)/(N-d)` (EFFR from the NY Fed); the expected move is spread over the two adjacent 25bp outcomes (the FedWatch step). 19 meetings qualify (2023-05 to 2026-09); 61 two-sided markets. Brier Kalshi .0077, futures .0070, difference +0.0007 [-0.0009,+0.0030]; held-out difference +0.0004 [-0.0007,+0.0018]. Almost every market is at 0-1c or 99c, so there is little to trade (held-out pure-futures rule at theta .02: +5.1c [-1.1,+11.2], 6 trades, 3 meetings). Efficient.
Live snapshot 2026-10-02 close (single date, no EV claim): Oct 28 hike 25bp futures-implied 0.20 vs Kalshi 0.17-0.18; Dec 9 hike 25bp 0.82 vs Kalshi 0.70-0.71 (ask .71, hold .26-.28). Sep 16 2026 meeting (resolved hike): futures 0.92 vs Kalshi ask .88 sixteen hours before. All three gaps have the same sign (futures above Kalshi), which could be a term premium in futures; that is the thing a forward log would settle. `results/fed_eval.txt`, `fed_sep2026.txt`; script `fed_futures.py`.

### The KXMORTGAGERATE ladder break
Found: KXMORTGAGERATE-27DEC30 (above X in any PMMS release published in 2027, max-over-year, early close allowed when a reading exceeds the strike, equal does not count, initial published value) T9.50 vs T9.75. Dutch book: YES T9.50 at 5c plus NO T9.75 at 94c, 925 lots, 915.75 dollars cost, 6.74 fees, locked until the end of 2027. Guaranteed profit 2.51 dollars = 0.27% over about 15 months (0.22% a year; a 4% T-bill earns about $45 on the same cash). The market is thin (volume 670/898, bids 2-17c, asks 56-62c at the 2026-10-04 read), the 925-lot depth at 5c has already gone. Rule text shows no quirk: both strikes share window, source and tie rule, so monotonicity is exactly required, and no settlement trap is visible (early close on a qualifying print only returns capital sooner). So it is real, not money. For the record I priced the whole ladder with a block bootstrap of PMMS weekly changes (`mortgage.py`): the verdict on fair values flips with the volatility window (2022-26 weekly sd .125 vs 2024-26 .082), e.g. T7.5 in 2026 fair .61 vs .40 against a .57/.62 book, so no edge claim on the ladder. PMMS is 7.28 on 2026-10-01 and 2026 max so far is 7.28.

## 2. Finance and crypto range markets

Option reference. Deribit public history does not reach back: trades are retrievable for about one day, and the order book is live only. So the historical test uses the Deribit volatility index DVOL (30-day ATM implied vol, hourly) as the options reference, lognormal with zero drift, at the snapshot time, and compares with (a) a vol scale fitted on train and (b) a time-of-day realised-variance model (mean realised variance of the same UTC window over the prior 30 days, from Coinbase 5-min returns). Settlement index: Kalshi settles on the 60-second average of CF Benchmarks BRTI/ERTI; spot at the snapshot is Coinbase, completed 5-minute candle (a first version leaked up to 5 minutes of the future; fixed). Indices use previous-close VIX/VXN. A live Deribit smile comparison is in `live_deribit_vs_kalshi.py`.

### Where the DVOL lognormal fails: intraday seasonality
BTC 4-hour return sd by UTC start hour over the last 60 days, as annualised vol: 17Z 21%, 21Z 25%, 01Z 27%, 05Z 45%, 09Z 38%, 13Z 54%. Kalshi's daily 5pm EDT close (21Z) is the quietest 4-hour window, and 30-minute events before the 10:00 EDT open are the most volatile. A flat DVOL lognormal is therefore too wide at 21Z (realised/model sd of the move 0.57) and too narrow at the 10:00 EDT open (1.22 for the 0.5h horizon). Monthly 5-minute realised vol vs DVOL: Jan 41 vs 41, Feb 70 vs 54, Mar 53 vs 54, Apr 38 vs 44, May 31 vs 38, Jun 53 vs 43, Jul 33 vs 38, Aug 36 vs 37, Sep 37 vs 37 (percent).

Trading toward DVOL, held-out: theta .02-.10 -2.0 to -10.6c (CIs mostly include 0). Trading toward the time-of-day model, held-out: -0.1 to -2.5c at 4h. Live Deribit snapshot 2026-10-04 (event KXBTCD-26OCT0417, 15.8h out, index 84,819): Deribit smile risk-neutral P(S>K) vs Kalshi mid at z -1.5..+1.6: Kalshi above Deribit by +3 to +13c for strikes below spot and below Deribit by 4 to 12c for strikes above spot (e.g. K=85,500: Kalshi .115, Deribit .238). So the live Kalshi book is a narrower distribution than the options market, consistent with the history (options overstate the realised move; DVOL was ~35 against ~21% realised in this window). Selling Kalshi tails to the Deribit curve would be the wrong way round: the options market is the rich one.

The one candidate cell (4h, buy NO = sell the upside, strike 1 to 2 DVOL-sigma above spot, mid about 8c): train (to 2026-08-27, 55 days) +4.56c [+1.7,+6.9], n=114; test (35 days) +4.70c [+2.1,+6.6], n=88, taker at the bid after fee. Neighbours: z>2 +1.7/+1.4c (always wins, tail risk unsampled), +.5..+1 +9.7/+7.8c (test CI [-2.3,+16.0]). Caveats that keep this at "shadow": all 90 days are one calm regime (DVOL 35-38, realised 33-37% overall and 21% in this window), the cell is also what a short-vol position earns until a vol spike, one +3% BTC move in four hours loses 100c on every filled lot in several strikes, and the cell was found on the same data as the test (the split protects only against time overfit, not cell selection). ## Shadows worth running (all log-only, no orders)
1. GDP vs GDPNow, 4 events a year: record the GDPNow normal and the book at 21:00Z the evening before each advance release, rule take at theta 0.05.
2. Fed decision vs ZQ futures: log daily futures-implied outcome probabilities (from `fed_futures.py`) next to the Kalshi book for the next 3 meetings. The goal is the missing history; the live gaps all point the same way.
3. NFP longshot YES (mid 15-35c): log book at T-15h for each print, 12 prints a year.
4. BTC upside-tail sell cell (z 1-2, 4h): log-only until it has seen a high-vol month; success criterion is the cell staying positive across a regime where DVOL exceeds 50. Taker-only evidence is what exists; the maker replay used the tape from the last 58 days.
No lane in this file clears capital; CPI, core CPI, Fed, S&P and the BTC-vs-options comparison say the Kalshi books are already at or inside the external references, and in the crypto/index cases it is the options market that is rich.

## Caveats
Displayed size is not in the compact candles; taker EV assumes top-of-book depth for 10 lots, which is unverified for thin series (ETH, Nasdaq, NFP). Historical-tier 1-minute candles are sparse for deep out-of-the-money strikes; the snapshot window there is up to 45 minutes. The CPI/core nowcast sd uses Cleveland's own error history, so the reference is deliberately non-optimistic, but a better-calibrated nowcast (for example one fitted to the 2021+ regime) would close part of the gap; it does not know consensus. Mid-price Brier scores at wide spreads (NFP, U3, YoY) include stale quotes; filter is spread <= 10c. FRED could not be queried (bot protection); PMMS came from Freddie Mac directly. KXJOBLESSCLAIMS candles and the full 200-day BTC historical pull were stopped before finishing (API contention); the BTC historical part covers Jun 28 to Aug 5. The data directory was not exposed to git: a local `data/.gitignore` containing `*`; scripts are backed up in the session scratch directory.

## Forward shadows: implementation and admission bars (added 2026-10-04, fixed in advance)
Runner `scripts/category-shadows.mjs` (`src/category-shadows/{gdp,fed,nfp,btc}.mjs`, tests `test/category-shadows.test.mjs`, GDPNow workbook reader `scripts/category-shadows-gdpnow.py`), run as the user unit `kalshi-category-shadows` every 300 s. Log-only: public reads, no credentials, no orders. State: `~/.local/state/561-group/settlements/forecasting-service/category-shadows/<family>/YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl` (plus `seen.jsonl` for the one-row-per-market-per-window dedup, `ticks.jsonl`, `results.jsonl`, `admission-readout.{json,md}`). Each row holds bid, ask and depth at the touch, the taker price and 10-lot fee of the take side, the reference and its inputs. `node scripts/category-shadows.mjs grade` fetches settlements and scores realised EV per contract (win minus taker price minus 10-lot fee) with a 4000-draw cluster bootstrap 95% CI.
Rules as logged (deviations from the record are marked):
- gdp: window opens 15.6h and closes 10h before the next KXGDP close (record: 21:00Z the evening before); reference 1 - Phi((X - GDPNow)/sd), sd = RMSE of the last 60 nowcast-vs-advance errors (1.16 today); take at theta 0.05; refuses a nowcast older than 10 days. Every market in the window is logged, takes flagged.
- fed: once per UTC day for the next 3 meetings; ZQ and EFFR chain as `fed_futures.py` (each later meeting's pre-rate is the previous meeting's implied post rate); take at theta 0.02 (record), added: taker price must lie in [5c, 95c] so near-certain 1-5c rows do not manufacture a tight CI.
- nfp: window 15.6h to 12h before close; spread <= 10c; buy YES at the ask when mid is in [0.15, 0.35). Reference is the book mid.
- btc: only rows in the cell are logged (high volume): 3.5 to 4h before a KXBTCD close, z = ln(X/spot)/(DVOL sqrt(T)) in [1, 2), spread <= 6c, mid in [1c, 99c], buy NO. Spot = Coinbase ticker, DVOL = latest 1-minute Deribit DVOL close. Logged beside it: DVOL lognormal P(YES), the same-time-of-day realised-variance lognormal (mean over the prior 30 days of summed squared 5-minute log returns in the same UTC window) as `pTod`/`zTod`. Cell selection is by DVOL z as in the record; the grader may later split by whether zTod agrees.
Admission bars (a family is read as "meets bar" only if every line holds; none of this moves money by itself):
- gdp: >= 8 settled advance releases, >= 20 would-take rows, mean EV >= +3c/contract, lower CI > 0 (clustered by release). Retire if mean <= 0 after 4 releases.
- fed: >= 8 settled meetings of which >= 3 ended in a hike or cut, >= 30 would-take rows, mean EV >= +1c, lower CI > 0 (clustered by meeting). Retire if mean <= 0 after 4 meetings.
- nfp: >= 30 settled prints with a would-take row, mean EV >= +5c, lower CI > 0 (clustered by print). Retire if mean <= 0 after 12 prints.
- btc: >= 60 settled KXBTCD events with a would-take row on >= 20 UTC days, including >= 7 UTC days whose rows carry DVOL > 50, mean EV >= +2c, lower CI > 0 (clustered by UTC day). Retire if mean <= 0 after 30 events.
Limits: the 5-minute tick samples the first tick inside each window, so the snapshot time is within 5 minutes of the rule time; displayed depth is the touch size only; the fed daily rows of one meeting share one outcome, which is why they are one cluster.
